# Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope — Launch-to-Science Research Dossier

## Research question

Why is the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope more than “Hubble with a wider
camera,” and how do its instrument design, survey program, and public data
system connect to the scientific conclusions astronomers hope to draw?

## Synthesis scaffold

### 1. Mission state

Roman has launched, but it has not yet produced the survey results described in
mission forecasts. The current phase is travel and commissioning. The first
images and the detailed start of science operations remain future milestones.

### 2. Observatory leverage

Roman’s distinctive capability is the combination of a 2.4-meter-class space
telescope with a large, stable focal plane and a 0.281-square-degree field of
view. The important comparison with Hubble is not “better resolution”; it is
Hubble-class sharpness applied to survey-scale areas and repeated observations.

### 3. Survey-to-inference chain

```text
Wide Field Instrument
→ wide, repeated imaging and slitless spectroscopy
→ shapes, positions, redshifts, light curves, and transient events
→ weak lensing + galaxy clustering + Type Ia supernovae + microlensing
→ constraints on structure growth, expansion history, and planet demographics
```

The final step is scientific inference, not direct observation. Roman observes
images and spectra; physical models and cross-checks turn those measurements
into constraints on dark energy, dark matter distribution, and exoplanet
populations.

### 4. Discovery system

Roman’s survey speed, high daily data volume, immediate public access, MAST
archive, and distributed processing responsibilities form one discovery
system. The archive and pipelines affect who can inspect, reproduce, combine,
and discover results in the data.

### 5. Instrument asymmetry

The Wide Field Instrument carries the mission’s primary survey science. The
Coronagraph Instrument is a technology demonstration. A useful wiki must keep
those roles separate instead of presenting both instruments as equivalent
sources of guaranteed science return.
